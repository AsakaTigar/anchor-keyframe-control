\begin{abstract}
Placing a user-specified image at an \emph{interior} time of a generated
video is usually treated as a scheduling problem: decide when to inject
the image's native condition. The schedule is the wrong unit of deployment: the same delayed
re-assertion family reduces interior anchor error by $28\%$ on CogVideoX's
concat interface, increases it by $93\%$ on VACE's mask interface, and is
locally vacuous on an LTX token interface whose swap response is
$0.040{\pm}0.009$. Worse, a fixed-response CogVideoX schedule tuned on DAVIS reverses on
Something-Something-v2 (SSv2), losing to
Native-Full in $54/54$ seeded runs (anchor LPIPS $0.872$ vs.\ $0.228$;
source-level paired difference $0.645$, BCa 95\% CI $[0.587,0.690]$).
ANCHOR turns this failure into a conservative controller: (1) a signed,
symmetric fixed-seed probe estimates target reachability and early
response--residual alignment; (2) confidence intervals classify the
interface--domain pair as \textsc{Wall}, \textsc{Persistent},
\textsc{Droppable}, or \textsc{Uncertain}; and (3) a native-full,
micro-skip, or log-SNR candidate is deployed only after a paired
target-domain calibration upper bound clears a non-inferiority margin.
Otherwise ANCHOR abstains to the audited Native-Full endpoint. The selector,
paired ridge estimator, manifest audit, and source-level inference are all
implemented. A completed five-source CogVideoX/DAVIS signed probe returns
\textsc{Uncertain} (mean $S=-27.2$, source-bootstrap BCa 95\% CI
$[-292.1,243.9]$). Separately, a locked CogVideoX/DAVIS 10{+}10 lane
passes promotion calibration and held-out val evaluation under the
paper's non-inferiority guard with $\delta_{\rm NI}{=}0$ (val mean
candidate-minus-native LPIPS $-0.154$, BCa 95\% CI
$[-0.202,-0.108]$, $10/10$ sources favor the candidate;
Table~\ref{tab:davis}).
SSv2 still has no executed candidate arm or signed target-domain probe, so
its audited replay in Table~\ref{tab:main} abstains and exactly reuses
Native-Full outputs; this is schedule identity, not an SSv2 adaptive win.
A benefit--disruption objective yields the sigmoid gate and the hard
skip-$K$ limit, while the calibration guard supports distributional---not
pointwise---non-inferiority. We measure the fixed-gate SSv2 failure and a
guarded DAVIS-domain candidate certificate; SSv2 certificates and true
online-feedback gains remain open. The revision's end-to-end test
(Sec.~
ef{sec:e2e}) exercises the full chain but ends in a correct
abstention; a positive case where the certified candidate beats
Native-Full on held-out sources is still missing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Image-to-video diffusion inherited a convenient convention: the reference
image sits at frame zero and generation rolls forward. Many practical
requests violate it. A creator specifies a contact event, a pose apex, a
product reveal, or a camera view at time $p$ in the middle of the clip and
asks the model to construct what plausibly happened before and after. The
condition becomes an \emph{interior keyframe}, and the task acquires a
vector of goals: execute the anchor at frame $p$, do not copy it elsewhere,
keep the seam around $p$ smooth, and keep the video moving.
Fig.~\ref{fig:motivation} previews the two failure modes this paper is
built around: the same schedule family flips sign across interfaces,
and it flips sign across domains even when the interface is fixed.

\paragraph{Boundary conditioning is not interior conditioning.}
Nearly every deployed image-to-video system conditions on the
\emph{first} frame: the reference image initializes the clip and the
sampler rolls forward, so the condition is consumed once at $t{=}0$ and
the remainder of the video is free-running. An \emph{interior keyframe}
asks for a different contract: the image must be executed at an
arbitrary time $p$ inside the clip, with plausible content generated
\emph{before} and \emph{after} it. The two contracts differ in what the
model is asked to do (initialize a trajectory vs.\ hit a target inside
one), in how failure appears (slow drift vs.\ a missed anchor or a
copied/leaked frame), and in what a schedule can act on: first-frame
conditioning can only be re-asserted along the rollout and its
influence at frame $p$ is \emph{propagated} through many steps, whereas
an interior interface exposes a per-step condition channel whose
response at $p$ may be strong, weak, or absent depending on the
interface. This paper studies the interior contract. First-frame
results appear only as a boundary baseline
(Tab.~\ref{tab:interfaces}, ``boundary'' rows) precisely to show that
propagated influence is a different estimand and must not be read as
interior reachability.

Recent training-free systems answer with \emph{scheduling}: decide at which
reverse steps to inject, drop, or re-assert the native
condition~\citep{lai2025flexti2v,jang2025frameguidance}. Underneath every
such schedule sit two assumptions that, to our knowledge, no prior work
tests: (i)~\emph{reachability}---the native interface can change the
requested frame at all; and (ii)~\emph{portability}---a schedule family
tuned on one interface--domain pair transfers elsewhere.

\paragraph{Both assumptions fail, measurably.}
We probe four open backbones with fixed-seed interventions: generate twice
with identical noise, prompt, and schedule, change only the anchor image,
and measure the response at the target frame relative to the anchor change
(Sec.~\ref{sec:diag}; conditioning sites and protocols per backbone are
disclosed in Tab.~\ref{tab:interfaces}). Three regimes emerge. On
CogVideoX's concat interface~\citep{yang2024cogvideox} the interior
channel executes anchors well, and skipping the first $K$ reverse steps
\emph{reduces} interior anchor error by up to $28\%$. VACE's mask
interface~\citep{jiang2025vace} responds to interior swaps almost
unit-for-unit ($0.954{\pm}0.029$), yet the \emph{same} skip family reverses
after a shallow optimum: $K{=}12$ increases anchor error by $93\%$ versus
full conditioning. Its off-target anchor distance moves from $0.29$ to
$0.94$, though we do not read that raw LPIPS distance as leakage. LTX-Video's token
interface~\citep{hacohen2025ltx} barely responds to interior conditions at
all ($0.040{\pm}0.009$): a local \emph{interface wall} where scheduling is
vacuous. One family, three opposite behaviors---the schedule is not the
object of interest; the \emph{interface response} is.

\paragraph{From failure to an executable conservative rule.}
We formalize the reverse process as a time-varying controlled system. A
per-step level $\gamma_s\in[0,1]$ interpolates the native condition between
its null path and the clean anchor. Linearization yields a target-response
operator $G_s$ and off-target operator $L_s$. ANCHOR measures their action
with symmetric paired probes, classifies only when confidence intervals
separate a regime, proposes the regime-specific schedule, and requires an
independent target-domain non-inferiority certificate before using any
non-native schedule. A wall, ambiguous probe, missing certificate, or failed
certificate maps to $\gamma\equiv1$. This extra domain guard is forced by
the evidence: the SSv2 failure~\citep{goyal2017something} occurs on the same CogVideoX concat interface
where the descriptive DAVIS~\citep{perazzi2016davis} fixed-schedule comparison has the opposite sign.

\paragraph{Why this explains the schedule literature.}
Fixed log-SNR gates---including hard skip-$K$---arise in the formulation as a
restricted open-loop special case (Lem.~\ref{lem:openloop}). The special
case holds only within its regime: within one interface, a threshold
$\tau$ calibrated at $T{=}30$ predicts the panel-mean optimal skip depth at
$T{=}50$ within $\pm2$ steps (predicted $K^\star{=}9$, observed $10$), and the same
fixed gate has lower observed anchor LPIPS at noncentral positions $12$
and $36$ on all $10$ clips at each position; across
interfaces the measured structure inverts (VACE) or weakens sharply (LTX),
and across domains it can reverse even with the interface fixed (DAVIS to
SSv2). Thus both the response probe and target-domain guard are necessary.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~A localized controllability formulation for native video-diffusion

(2)~An implemented probe--classify--gate selector with an explicit
\textsc{Uncertain} state and a target-domain paired non-inferiority guard;
every unsupported branch abstains to the bit-audited Native-Full endpoint.
(3)~A benefit--disruption derivation of the log-SNR sigmoid and skip-$K$
limit, plus an optional leakage-regularized QP and residual-feedback toolkit

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Arbitrary-time image conditioning.}
FlexTI2V inserts images at arbitrary positions via position-aware noise
swapping~\citep{lai2025flexti2v}; Frame Guidance optimizes frame-level
losses through the predicted clean video~\citep{jang2025frameguidance};
Flowception interleaves observed frames through variable-length flow
matching~\citep{ifriqi2025flowception}; VACE and LTX expose native
keyframe interfaces~\citep{jiang2025vace,hacohen2025ltx}; SmartDirector
and KeyFrame-Compass push multi-keyframe narratives and execution-centric
evaluation~\citep{zhang2026smartdirector,tang2026keyframecompass}. These
works establish the task; none measures whether the interface it uses
reaches the requested frame, nor penalizes temporal leakage as a control cost.
\paragraph{Condition scheduling in diffusion.}
RePaint re-asserts known regions~\citep{lugmayr2022repaint}, SDEdit picks
an entry noise level~\citep{meng2022sdedit}, posterior sampling injects
measurement gradients~\citep{chung2023dps}, and classifier-free guidance
scales condition strength~\citep{ho2022cfg}. All are open-loop in our
taxonomy; our linearized formulation represents their timing decisions as
restricted schedules on a measured response map.
\paragraph{Evaluation.}
LPIPS~\citep{zhang2018lpips} cannot see temporal mis-localization; we
follow VBench/VBench++ and VideoScore in keeping quality axes
disentangled~\citep{huang2024vbench,huang2024vbenchpp,he2024videoscore}
and use anchor-specific execution diagnostics on the executed
DAVIS~\citep{perazzi2016davis}, UCF-101~\citep{soomro2012ucf101}, and
SSv2~\citep{goyal2017something} panels. EPIC-KITCHENS, RealEstate10K, and
Vimeo-90K remain planned rather than reported
evidence~\citep{damen2022epic,zhou2018realestate,xue2019vimeo}.

\section{Interior-Keyframe Generation as Local Control}
\label{sec:form}
We now make the informal picture precise.
Let $G_\theta$ be a frozen video diffusion model with a native image
interface. Given anchor $a$, prompt $q$, requested frame $p$, seed $\xi$,
and $T$ reverse steps, the output is
$\hat x=G_\theta(a,q,p,\xi;\gamma)$ with schedule
$\gamma=(\gamma_T,\ldots,\gamma_1)\in[0,1]^T$ acting on the native
condition
\begin{equation}
c_s(\gamma_s)=c_\emptyset+\gamma_s\bigl(c(a)-c_\emptyset\bigr),
\label{eq:native}
\end{equation}
where $c_\emptyset$ is the model's \emph{own} null path (unconditional,
dropped-condition, or all-masked); a zero tensor is admitted only if a unit
test shows equivalence. For each backbone we fix the interpolation site in
condition-latent space and verify both endpoints: $\gamma{=}1$ must
reproduce the official pipeline bit-compatibly and $\gamma{=}0$ its null
path. On CogVideoX the $\gamma{=}1$ test passes exactly (LPIPS
$0.000000$, max pixel difference $0$, $3$ clips $\times$ paired
generations); backbones failing these tests are excluded rather than
patched.

\subsection{Target response and off-target leakage}
Writing one reverse update as $z_{s-1}=F_s(z_s,c_s)$ with Jacobians
$A_s=\partial F_s/\partial z_s$, $B_s=\partial F_s/\partial c_s$, a
first-order expansion around a reference schedule $\bar\gamma$ gives
\begin{equation}
\delta z_0=\sum_{s=1}^{T}\Bigl(\prod_{r=1}^{s-1}A_r\Bigr)B_s\,\Delta c\,
\delta\gamma_s+r(\delta\gamma),
\label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta c=c(a)-c_\emptyset$; the product follows implementation
order (step $s$ propagated through steps $s{-}1,\ldots,1$).
\begin{assumption}[Local smoothness]\label{ass:smooth}
There is $\kappa_F<\infty$ with
$\|r(\delta\gamma)\|\le(\kappa_F/2)\|\delta\gamma\|^2$ on the probe tube
$\{\|\delta\gamma\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_0\}$.
\end{assumption}
Let $P_p$ select the requested frame, $P_{\neg p}$ the rest, and $\phi$ a
fixed feature map. Define, for the given $(a,q,\xi)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
G_s&=J_\phi P_p\Bigl(\prod_{r=1}^{s-1}A_r\Bigr)B_s\Delta c,\\
L_s&=J_\phi P_{\neg p}\Bigl(\prod_{r=1}^{s-1}A_r\Bigr)B_s\Delta c.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:GL}
\end{equation}
Fixed-noise finite differences estimate their action without
differentiating through the sampler. We deliberately report the
\emph{trajectory-level} estimand $G_s(a,q,\xi)$ and its variability across
seeds, rather than an unidentified population average.

\subsection{Diagnostics: two curves, not one scalar}
For a normalized anchor swap $a\to a'$ define per-step target and leakage
magnitudes $r_s,u_s$ and the summaries
$R=\sum_s w_s r_s^2$ (reachability mass) and
$U=\sum_s w_s u_s^2$ (leakage energy), $\sum_s w_s{=}1$. A localized
controllability index
$\mathrm{LCI}=R/(R+\lambda U+\epsilon)$ is reported \emph{only if}
$R\ge 3\hat\sigma^2_{\mathrm{probe}}$, the null-swap noise floor;
this validity gate prevents the classic $0/0$ pathology in which a weak
interface scores as a localized one.

\begin{proposition}[Local interface wall]\label{prop:wall}
If $G_s=0$ for all $s$, then $J_\phi P_p\,\delta z_0=0$ to first order for
every schedule perturbation: no local re-assertion schedule can change the
tested target feature through this interface; any measured gain must be
higher-order, use a different feature/intervention, or modify the model.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $G_s\equiv 0$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:linear} and keep first-order
terms.
\end{proof}
The proposition is intentionally elementary; its empirical force comes
from the estimator. We declare a wall only when the upper confidence bound
for $R$ lies below $3\times$ the null-swap floor, and only after a
hook-isolation audit excludes implementation error as the cause.

\section{ANCHOR: Probe, Classify, Gate}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Stage 1: signed paired probe}
ANCHOR calibrates an \emph{interface--domain pair}, not a backbone name in
isolation. Fixed-seed anchor swaps estimate reachability against a null-swap
noise floor. To estimate a signed response map, an interior reference
schedule is perturbed symmetrically:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat x_j^\pm&=\hat x(\bar\gamma\pm\varepsilon v_j),\\
Y_j&=\frac{\phi(\hat x_j^+)-\phi(\hat x_j^-)}{2\varepsilon},\\
v_j&\in\{-1,1\}^T .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pairedprobe}
\end{equation}
The paired design removes the shared stochastic trajectory and avoids the
one-sided clipping bias incurred when perturbing a box boundary. Ridge
regression with a second-difference penalty estimates $G$ and $L$; probing
continues until the stability bound below clears a frozen tolerance. A fixed
``ten probe'' budget is not justified by our simulation: held-out recovery
remains poor at $m{=}8$ and improves materially through $m{=}24$.

\paragraph{Probe cost and certificate caching.}
Calibrating one interface--domain pair costs $2m$ generator calls (two
per Rademacher pair, no back-propagation; $2m{=}48$ at $m{=}24$) plus
the fixed evaluation generations on calibration sources. This is a
one-off cost per certificate, not a per-request cost: a certificate is
keyed by model revision, condition site, sampler, domain, metric, and
candidate vector, so a deployment service probes once per key and
serves later requests from the stored certificate; any key change
invalidates it and reverts requests to Native-Full until a new probe
completes. Per-request overhead therefore equals the native pipeline
plus a lookup, and the probe cost amortizes over the certificate's
lifetime. Peak memory is dominated by the backbone; the probe adds one
paired forward pass at a time and stores only schedule vectors and the
recovered response maps ($O(T)$ and $O(dT)$).

Let $K_0=\lceil T/6\rceil$, $g_{\rm early}=\sum_{s\le K_0}G_s$, and let
$e_0$ be the target residual under the null continuation. The signed
alignment statistic is
\begin{equation}
S=\langle g_{\rm early},e_0\rangle .
\label{eq:alignment}
\end{equation}
Bootstrap intervals are computed over calibration sources. A stored norm
$\|G_s\|$ cannot recover $S$ and is therefore invalid for classification.

\subsection{Stage 2: confidence-aware classification}
Write $R_{\rm lo},R_{\rm hi}$ and $S_{\rm lo},S_{\rm hi}$ for confidence
bounds and $h=3\hat\sigma^2_{\rm probe}$. ANCHOR uses
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{regime}=\begin{cases}
\textsc{Wall}, & R_{\rm hi}<h,\\
\textsc{Persistent}, & R_{\rm lo}\ge h,\ S_{\rm lo}>0,\\
\textsc{Droppable}, & R_{\rm lo}\ge h,\ S_{\rm hi}\le0,\\
\textsc{Uncertain}, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:classify}
\end{equation}
An invalid, unsigned, one-sided, or non-identifiable probe is
\textsc{Uncertain}. This fourth state is operationally essential: uncertainty
must not be silently converted into a favorable regime.

\paragraph{Why a conservative fallback is a product feature.}
For a consumer authoring product, \textsc{Uncertain} is not an
embarrassing null result; it is the state that makes the system
shippable. First, it keeps \emph{quality monotone with evidence}: the
tool emits the audited Native-Full video exactly when the interface--
domain pair has not been certified, so a user never receives a
regression that the system cannot justify. Second, it bounds
\emph{cost and latency}: abstention deploys no probe-specific schedule
and no candidate generation, so the worst-case request costs the same
as the native pipeline plus the one-off certificate lookup. Third, it
gives the interface a \emph{stable contract} across model revisions:
a certificate is bound to a model revision, condition site, sampler,
domain, and candidate vector (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}), and any change
invalidates it back to \textsc{Uncertain} rather than silently
consulting a stale schedule. Fourth, it is \emph{auditable}: every
request carries a machine-readable verdict
(\textsc{Wall}/\textsc{Persistent}/\textsc{Droppable}/
\textsc{Uncertain}) and, when deployed, the bound that justified the
candidate, which is what a consumer platform needs for incident review
and for explaining an edit outcome to a creator. Finally, it converts
an unverifiable gamble into a \emph{product decision}: teams can ship
Native-Full today, collect certificates per interface--domain pair,
and promote a candidate only when its own calibration bound clears the
margin---an operating model that is compatible with staged rollout and
A/B testing. The alternative---deploying an uncertified schedule and
hoping it transfers---is exactly the failure mode measured on SSv2
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), where three fixed schedules lose on all $54$
seeded runs while the native endpoint does not move.

\subsection{Stage 3: propose, certify, or abstain}
The regime proposes one schedule:
\begin{equation}
\tilde\gamma_s=\begin{cases}
1, & \textsc{Wall}\text{ or }\textsc{Uncertain},\\
\mathbb 1[s>1], & \textsc{Persistent},\\
\sigma\!\left(\alpha(\lsnr_s-\tau)\right), & \textsc{Droppable}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:regimegate}
\end{equation}
The persistent candidate is the observed micro-skip $K{=}1$; the droppable
candidate freezes $(\alpha,\tau)$ before target-domain evaluation. Neither
non-native candidate is deployed directly. On independent target-domain
calibration sources, we average seeds within each source and define
$\Delta_i=\mathcal L_i(\tilde\gamma)-\mathcal L_i(\mathbf1)$. Let
$U_{1-\eta}$ be a one-sided source-bootstrap upper bound for
$\mathbb E[\Delta]$. The deployed gate is
\begin{equation}
\gamma^{\method}=\begin{cases}
\tilde\gamma, & U_{1-\eta}\le\delta_{\rm NI},\\
\mathbf1, & \text{missing/failed certificate},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:guard}
\end{equation}
with conservative default $\delta_{\rm NI}=0$. The certificate is bound to
the model revision, condition site, sampler, domain, metric, and candidate
vector in the run manifest. This domain guard is the correction required by
the DAVIS-to-SSv2 reversal.

\subsection{Benefit--disruption derivation}
At step $s$, let $B_s\ge0$ be terminal binding benefit and $D_s\ge0$ prior
disruption. The separable surrogate
\begin{equation}
J_s(\gamma_s)=(1-\gamma_s)^2B_s+\gamma_s^2D_s
\label{eq:tradeoff}
\end{equation}
has minimizer
$\gamma_s^\star=B_s/(B_s+D_s)$ when $B_s+D_s>0$. For $B_s,D_s>0$ this is
$\sigma(\log B_s-\log D_s)$, with zero endpoints obtained by continuity.
A local log-linear model
$\log(B_s/D_s)=\alpha(\lsnr_s-\tau)$ yields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:regimegate}; when both terms vanish, ANCHOR abstains rather
than assigning an arbitrary optimum.

\begin{proposition}[Hard skip as the sharp-gate limit]\label{prop:zero}
If $\lsnr_s$ is strictly increasing and
$\tau\in(\lsnr_K,\lsnr_{K+1})$, then as $\alpha\to\infty$,
$\sigma(\alpha(\lsnr_s-\tau))\to\mathbb 1[s>K]$ pointwise, i.e.\ the
first $K$ execution-order steps are skipped.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The sigmoid argument tends to $-\infty$ below $\tau$ and $+\infty$ above
it; strict monotonicity excludes equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Guarded calibration non-inferiority]\label{prop:guard}
For a fixed candidate and realized calibration sample, Eq.~\eqref{eq:guard}
either returns Native-Full exactly or deploys a candidate whose reported
one-sided bound is at most $\delta_{\rm NI}$. If sources are i.i.d. from the
deployment domain and the bootstrap bound has coverage $1-\eta$, then the
same confidence statement transfers to the domain mean.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The fallback branch is identical to Native-Full. The deployment branch is
entered only when its computed bound passes the stated inequality; the
population statement is exactly the assumed coverage event.
\end{proof}
This is a distributional calibration guarantee, not pointwise immunity to
domain shift. The fixed SSv2 failure rules out the stronger ``never worse on
every request'' claim.

\subsection{Optional linearized schedule optimizer}
Let $e=\phi(a)-\phi(\hat x_p(\bar\gamma))$ be the target residual under
the reference schedule, $G=[G_1,\ldots,G_T]$, $L$ the stacked leakage
operator, $D$ the first-difference matrix, and
$\mathcal B=\{\delta\gamma: 0\le\bar\gamma+\delta\gamma\le 1\}$. The
candidate \method\ formulation solves
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\delta\gamma\in\mathcal B}\quad
&\tfrac12\|e-G\delta\gamma\|_W^2
+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|L\delta\gamma\|_2^2\\
&+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\|D(\bar\gamma+\delta\gamma)\|_2^2
+\tfrac{\mu_0}{2}\|\delta\gamma\|_2^2 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:qp}
\end{equation}
The first three terms represent missed anchor, copy/freeze leakage, and
brittle switching; the final ridge makes numerical identifiability explicit.
\begin{theorem}[Unique optimum of the linearized objective]\label{thm:qp}
Let $Q=G^\top WG+\lambda L^\top L+\beta D^\top D+\mu_0 I$ with any ridge
$\mu_0>0$ (the implementation scales it to the unridged Hessian; without it,
$Q\succ0$ iff $\mathbf 1\notin\ker(WG)\cap\ker L$, since
$\ker D^\top D=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$). Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:qp} has a
unique solution; without box constraints
$\delta\gamma^{\star}=Q^{-1}(G^\top We-\beta D^\top D\bar\gamma)$, and with
them the optimum is the $Q$-metric projection of $\delta\gamma^\star$ onto
$\mathcal B$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Strict convexity with Hessian $Q\succ0$; stationarity gives the closed
form; the box case is
$\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal B}\|x-\delta\gamma^\star\|_Q$, i.e.\ the
$Q$-projection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Closed-form residual-feedback candidate}
Solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:qp} needs the full response map. The feedback form
needs only the current trajectory. At checkpoint $s$ we adopt the
\emph{null-continuation reference}: executed steps keep their realized
gates and all remaining steps default to $\gamma{=}0$, so the decision
variable is the absolute injection level and
$e_s=\psi(z_a)-\psi(\hat z_{0|s,p})$ is the residual if injection stopped
now. This convention yields a one-coordinate receding-horizon surrogate of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:qp}; mixing it with a nonzero-reference residual systematically
over-injects (we verified both readings in simulation; the wrong one loses
even to zero injection). With the complete two-sided difference penalty,
each fixed-zero adjacent edge contributes another quadratic denominator
term; the lightweight rule retains only the realized-neighbor edge and is
not the exact full-QP coordinate solve.
With calibrated per-step response direction $g_s$ and leakage energy
$\ell_s^2$,
\begin{equation}
\min_{0\le\gamma\le1}
\tfrac12\|e_s-\gamma g_s\|_2^2
+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\gamma^2\ell_s^2
+\tfrac{\rho_s}{2}(\gamma-\gamma_{s+1})^2
\label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
has the exact solution
\begin{equation}
\gamma_s^{\star}=\proj\!\left(
\frac{\langle g_s,e_s\rangle+\rho_s\gamma_{s+1}}
{\|g_s\|_2^2+\lambda\ell_s^2+\rho_s}
\right).
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
Corrective alignment increases injection; weak interfaces, high leakage,
and schedule roughness shrink it; negative alignment shuts it off. The
proposed residual would be decoded every $R$ steps and interpolated between
checkpoints. This online update was not implemented in the reported video
experiment; its Fixed-Response Gate instead used one precomputed schedule
for every source and seed.
\begin{proposition}[One-step descent]\label{prop:descent}
Let $a_s=[\langle g_s,e_s\rangle+\rho_s\gamma_{s+1}]_+$,
$d_s=\|g_s\|^2+\lambda\ell_s^2+\rho_s$, and suppose the true local
objective is upper-bounded by the surrogate plus $\kappa_s\gamma^2/2$
(Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth} gives
$\kappa_s\le\kappa_F\|g_s\|C_\psi$). Using denominator $d_s+\kappa_s$
with $a_s\le d_s+\kappa_s$,
$\tilde J_s(\gamma_s^\star)-\tilde J_s(0)\le -a_s^2/\bigl(2(d_s+\kappa_s)\bigr)$.
\end{proposition}
In practice $\kappa_s$ is replaced by a bootstrap upper confidence bound
from a probe-radius sweep; the guarantee holds when the UCB covers the
truth.

\subsection{Response-map stability and stopping rule}
Rademacher probes $v^{(j)}\in\{\pm1\}^T$ around $\bar\gamma$ yield paired
differences $\Delta y^{(j)}\approx Gv^{(j)}$; we recover smooth curves by
the ridge estimator
$\widehat G=YV^\top(VV^\top+\mu D_2^\top D_2)^{-1}$
($D_2$: second difference), provided the kernel condition checked in
the supplement holds. Estimation
error propagates to the controller as follows.
\begin{theorem}[Calibration stability]\label{thm:stability}
If $Q\succeq\mu I$, $\mu>0$, and $\|\Delta Q\|_2<\mu$, then the estimated
unconstrained controller satisfies
\begin{equation}
\|\widehat{\delta\gamma}-\delta\gamma^\star\|_2\le
\frac{\|\Delta b\|_2+\|\Delta Q\|_2\|\delta\gamma^\star\|_2}
{\mu-\|\Delta Q\|_2}.
\label{eq:stability}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$(Q+\Delta Q)(\widehat{\delta\gamma}-\delta\gamma^\star)=\Delta b-\Delta
Q\,\delta\gamma^\star$ and
$\sigma_{\min}(Q+\Delta Q)\ge\mu-\|\Delta Q\|_2$.
\end{proof}
The box-constrained extension requires care because the two projections
live in different metrics; the supplement states the extra
$\sqrt{\kappa(Q)}$ and metric-perturbation terms explicitly. Probing stops
when a bootstrap upper bound on the right-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:stability} falls below a frozen tolerance
$\epsilon_\gamma$; if that fails within the probe budget, a future online
controller would be reported as unsupported on that interface.

\subsection{Open-loop gates as a special case}
\begin{lemma}[Open-loop reduction]\label{lem:openloop}
If $e_s,g_s,\ell_s$ are deterministic functions of $\lsnr_s$ and
$h(\lsnr_s)=\langle g,e\rangle/(\|g\|^2+\lambda\ell^2+\rho)$ is monotone,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} is a monotone schedule in $\lsnr$; a logistic fit
recovers the fixed sigmoid family, and hard skip-$K$ is its
$\alpha\to\infty$ limit.
\end{lemma}
The lemma predicts both sides of the evidence: \emph{within} an interface
whose response is a stable function of $\lsnr$, a frozen threshold should
transfer across sampler discretizations---and it does
(Sec.~\ref{sec:diag}); \emph{across} interfaces with different response
structure, a fixed gate need not transfer---as the executed diagnostics and
SSv2 stress test demonstrate.

\section{End-to-end Pipeline Test}
\label{sec:e2e}
The submission validated components in isolation; this revision closes
the loop on a small, disclosed case (three calibration + three held-out
DAVIS sources, CogVideoX-5B-I2V, $T{=}30$, $p{=}24$, one prompt, seed
42; released reference implementation). The signed paired probe
($m{=}4$ pairs per source) classified the pair as \textsc{Persistent}
($S{=}{+}0.26/{+}1.18/{+}1.40$; $R{=}0.211/1.312/1.465$; $S$ lower
bound $0.262>0$), and the regime automatically proposed the
micro-skip $K{=}1$ candidate---no schedule was nominated by hand. On
the three calibration sources the candidate-minus-native anchor-LPIPS
deltas were $+0.034/-0.036/-0.056$; the one-sided source-bootstrap
upper bound was $+0.011>\delta_{\rm NI}{=}0$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:guard}
abstained and the deployed output is exactly Native-Full. A post-veto
diagnostic (not a deployment) evaluated the vetoed candidate on the
three held-out sources: it was worse on all three ($\Delta$LPIPS
$+0.096/+0.377/+0.049$; mean $+0.174$; $\Delta$DINOv2 $-0.431$), so
the veto was protective rather than costly. The pipeline therefore
executes end-to-end---probe, classify, propose, certify, abstain---but
it does not produce an adaptive win on this panel; a positive E2E case
remains open and is listed in the limitations. Cost: $30$ generations
($24$ probe $+$ $6$ guard), $5{,}521$\,s wall-clock on one A100-80\,GB,
$164.4{\pm}1.0$\,s per generation, $37.5$\,GB peak; certificates
amortize this one-off cost (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).

\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Practice}
\looseness=-1
A keyframe underdetermines its surrounding video. The interface panel is
small and mostly single-prompt/single-seed; only SSv2 has $27$ sources and two
seeds. E4 produced a genuine \textsc{Uncertain} verdict (CI crosses zero; 3/5
sources negative, 2/5 positive), demonstrating the fourth classifier state
is empirically reachable, not merely theoretical. E4 covers only five
DAVIS sources and only the signed $S$ component; $R$, VACE/SSv2 signed
probes, target-domain candidates, the $2{\times}2$ control, Frame
Guidance, QP, and online feedback remain unexecuted, so the adaptive row
is an identity audit.
Off-target anchor distance and gray-frame difference
are not validated leakage or motion, and synthetic results are not video
evidence. Public datasets may contain identifiable people and copyrighted
media; we follow licenses, release manifests/metrics rather than restricted
media, document removals, and gate human evaluation on institutional review.
\emph{Practitioner takeaway}: for interior-keyframe editing, first test
whether the exact native interface reaches the requested frame, then
certify the proposed schedule against Native-Full on the target domain; if
either test is missing or ambiguous, use Native-Full.

\paragraph{No human perceptual study in this revision.}
All headline numbers are automatic: anchor LPIPS at the target frame
and source-level bootstrap intervals. They measure distance to the
requested image, not perceived identity, motion naturalness, or
authoring acceptability. We did not run a mean-opinion-score study, so
no claim in this paper should be read as a perceptual preference; a
preregistered study (identity, motion, perceived drift; $n$, scale,
confidence intervals, inter-rater agreement) remains future work and
its absence is a key limitation. The same limitation applies to
temporal quality: anchor LPIPS cannot see flicker, frame leakage, or
motion collapse. Clip-level temporal-consistency checks (adjacent-frame
embedding similarity and flow-based smoothing, following the
VBench/VBench++ axes) are specified in the released evaluation code,
but they were \emph{not} executed on the ANCHOR panels in this
revision; no motion-quality claim is made from them, and running them
on the released videos is a listed next step.

\section{Conclusion}
\looseness=-1
Concat, mask, and token interfaces respond in qualitatively different ways,
and CogVideoX's SSv2 stress test shows that a schedule can reverse across
domains even with the interface fixed ($0/54$ wins; $3.83\times$
mean anchor LPIPS). ANCHOR therefore probes, classifies only with separated
confidence intervals, and certifies on-domain before deviating from
Native-Full.
The completed five-source DAVIS signed probe returns \textsc{Uncertain}, not
a favorable regime; the fallback is implemented exactly, while nontrivial
adaptive and online video gains remain open empirical requirements.
